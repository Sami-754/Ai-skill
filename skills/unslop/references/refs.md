# References

Finished screens by Damir Sokolovsky ([@heydamir](https://x.com/heydamir)). Review these visual patterns before the Visuals pass. Take their qualities, never their subject, palette or layout, so every product still gets its own screen.

| Reference pattern | What it shows | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero scene | An invoicing hero with a paper plane drawn in digits and currency signs, small notes pinned to it, and the product rising from below | The visual is made of the product's own material, money, so it is the moment and the signature at once. Text sits on calm sky, the chrome is quiet, and the product view invites a scroll |
| Dashboard scene | A finance dashboard with two rendered payment cards and a revenue chart drawn as soft colored curves over a mirrored grey comparison | Quiet graphite chrome, and all the color lives in two moments, a real looking object and a chart where every color means a series |
| Editorial print hero | An incident monitoring hero drawn as one blue engraving of a lighthouse on a rocky coast, with a serif headline in the same ink | A print style working because the whole page is in it. One ink, one paper tone, and the headline sits in the calm sky the drawing leaves for it |
| Stylized 3D footer | A footer as a soft 3D meadow with a few matte crystal shapes and a huge wordmark standing behind the hills | Friendly stylized 3D with a restrained palette, type that becomes part of the scene, and quiet links on the open sky |

What they share: one bright element per screen, quiet chrome around it, a visual that can be explained in one sentence, and headlines placed on space that was left empty for them.
